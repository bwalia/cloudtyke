A stadium is built once.

I worked at Ticketmaster, so I've spent a lot of time thinking about stadiums. And the more I work with cloud platforms, the more I think a stadium is the best way to explain one.

On Saturday it hosts a football match. Next week, a cricket Test. After that, a concert. The crew swaps the pitch overnight. The gates, the seats, the security checks and the ground rules don't change at all.

That's a cloud landing zone.

The platform team builds the stadium once:
🚪 Gates = networking (every request comes in the same way and gets checked)
🪑 Seats and stands = standard, shared capacity
🛡️ Security = the same for every event, never negotiable
📋 Rules = governance and policy, applied to the whole ground

Then app teams bring their "event", whether a new app, a migrated one or a rebuilt one, and play on the pitch. They move fast because nobody has to rebuild the gates first.

None of this is new, either. The Colosseum added an underground level of lifts and trapdoors around AD 90 so whole sets could rise into the arena: a hidden platform that made every show quicker. Cricket grounds like the MCG grow their pitches outside and crane them in. Tottenham's grass pitch slides away in three parts to reveal an NFL surface. One venue, many events, fast changeovers.

So we built CloudTyke Arena: a free, interactive stadium where you click the gates, stands, control room and pitch to see what each one is in Azure, AWS and GCP. Then you solve nine short puzzle chapters, starting with assembling a whole landing zone piece by piece.

There's also a deeper Azure landing zones section: the management group hierarchy, hub-and-spoke vs Virtual WAN, subscription vending, and walkthroughs for starting fresh, migrating from on-premises and upgrading an existing estate.
